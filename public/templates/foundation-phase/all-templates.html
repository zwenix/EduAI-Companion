How to award this in class

  • Print at 100 % on A4 colour (or 2 sheets per page for desk copies) and hand it over in front of the class — Foundation Phase learners need the audience.
  • Read the citation aloud so the whole class hears exactly which behaviour is being praised, e.g. "for excellent achievement in Mathematics".
  • Photograph or scan signed awards into the learner portfolio of evidence; CAPS requires records of recognition to travel with the class file.
  • For Grade R, record the award as a Level 4 (Fully competent) comment on the criterion-related checklist rather than a percentage.

🤝 Inclusive education — every learner can shine

  • Learners who struggle academically can receive this certificate for a specific, observable strength (neat handwriting, helping a friend read, finishing all 5 tasks).
  • Read the certificate aloud and pair it with a tactile star sticker for learners with a visual impairment.
  • Translate the personal message into the learner's home language at the bottom so caregivers at the parent meeting can share the moment.

👪 Note to parents / caregivers

  • Ten minutes a night, five nights — that is all. Longer reading at this age turns readers off.
  • Three ways to help, in order of value: (1) listen to your child read aloud; (2) read TO your child; (3) talk about the story.
  • If your child is stuck on a word for more than 5 seconds, TELL them the word and keep moving — fluency and enjoyment matter more than decoding tonight.
  • Sign each night. A signed log is a record for the teacher, not a grade.

Early-warning box (teacher)

  • Fewer than 3 of 5 nights, two weeks running → phone the caregiver (log the call) and offer a 15-minute homework club.
  • Learner has no exercise book, not no homework → escalate through the class teacher to the HOD and check the DBE school-nutrition / stationery support lists.
